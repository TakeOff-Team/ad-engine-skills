# Ad copy digest — the parts of direct response that fit on a static ad

Distilled for 3–9 word headlines on a 4:5 canvas. Long-form techniques (slippery slide, bucket brigades, founder story) are deliberately left out — there is no "next sentence" on a static ad. Sources: Schwartz, Hopkins, Ogilvy, Halbert, Caples, Sugarman, Collier, via the house `direct-response-copy` skill.

## Awareness — decide this first (Schwartz)

The stage decides what the headline is allowed to be about.

| Stage | The reader | Headline job | Eligible formulas |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | doesn't know they have the problem | connect to identity or a scene; don't sell | story, scene, "do you ever…", transformation-as-story |
| **Problem-aware** | feels the pain, doesn't know solutions exist | name the pain more precisely than they can | question, warning/mistake, pain-quantified specificity |
| **Solution-aware** | knows solutions exist, not yours | show the mechanism is different | reason-why, "without [common pain]", contrarian |
| **Product-aware** | knows you, hasn't bought | proof, differentiator, objection | specificity with a number, testimonial result, comparison |
| **Most-aware** | wants it, needs a push | the offer, the guarantee, the price | direct statement, deal, risk-reversal |

Less aware → more scene, less product. Most aware → the fewest words. A batch that sits entirely at one stage tests one hypothesis N times.

## Headline formulas (pick by stage; use a different one per variant)

- **Master formula** — `[action verb] + [specific outcome] + [timeframe or contrast]`. *"3–5 new accounts a month, or you don't pay."* Contrast version does before/after in six words.
- **Specificity** (Ogilvy) — `[specific number] + [unexpected detail]`; the reader draws the conclusion. *"Booked 41 estimates. Hired nobody."* Shows, never says.
- **Question** (Cody, ran 40 years) — a question they're already asking themselves. `Do you [struggle]?` / `Still [undesirable state]?` / `What if [outcome]?` Self-selects the reader.
- **Transformation** — `From [bad state] to [good state]`. The reader sees themselves in the before.
- **Story** (Caples) — `They [doubted] when I [action]… but when I [result]`. Complete arc, universal emotion; works unaware.
- **Reason-why** (Hopkins) — `Why [surprising thing happens]`. *"Why the phone stops ringing in July."*
- **Warning / mistake** — `Don't [action] until [condition]` / `[N] mistakes [audience] make`. Loss beats gain.
- **Contrarian** — `Stop [common advice]` / `[belief] is wrong`. Must deliver; contrarian-for-its-own-sake backfires.
- **Direct statement** — the offer, plainly. Only for the most-aware. *"We run your ads. You pay the ad spend."*
- **Number** — odd beats even, specific beats round (37 > 40). Stops the scroll; promises bounded value.

**Why headlines fail** (Caples, decades of tests): clever instead of clear · no self-interest · vague instead of specific ("save $327" beats "save money") · curiosity with no benefit (clickbait that disappoints) · everything told, nothing left to want. Test at least five — one will outpull the rest 2–10×.

## Pain: quantify it or stage it

**Do the math.** *"4 hrs on emails + 6 on the landing page + 4 on Stripe + ∞ overthinking = 22+ hours."* A number they can weigh against your price. Only with documented inputs.

**Or make the scene.** *"You and your team both reply to the urgent email. Best case you look disorganized. Worst case you contradict each other."* They've been there; now they feel it instead of acknowledging it. Needs no proof — it's their memory.

**The So-What chain.** Feature → *so what?* → functional → *so what?* → financial → *so what?* → emotional. Copy lives at the bottom. Not "saves 4 hours" but "close the laptop at 5 instead of 9." Not "automates outreach" but "wake up to replies." Three levels down, then write.

## Testimonials — structure, not sentiment

`[before state] + [action] + [specific outcome] + [timeframe] + [emotional reaction]`. *"Went from chasing every lead to turning work away. First estimate booked the same afternoon."* "4 months" is believable; "helped me succeed" is not. On a card: verbatim, `<mark>` on the payoff phrase, name, context, source, date. Never a face + name + quote that isn't one real person.

## CTAs — describe the benefit, not the action

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Learn more | See how the phone starts ringing |
| Sign up | Send me the first lesson free |
| Book now | Book a 15-min call |
| Buy now | Start building |

Friction reducer under it when there's room: `[risk reversal] + [social proof] + [speed]` — *"No retainer. 40+ service businesses. Live in a week."* — every element documented.

## Voice: talk in their terms

- Their vocabulary from the VoC quotes, even when it's plainer than the brand's. "The phone isn't ringing" beats "lead generation."
- "You" and "I". Contractions. Fragments allowed. Opinions without hedging.
- One idea per line. A headline is not a sentence with commas.
- Specific > general, always. A competitor's name should never fit in your headline unchanged.

## AI tells — instant disqualifiers on an ad

**Words:** delve · dive into · comprehensive · robust · cutting-edge · utilize · leverage · crucial · vital · essential · unlock · unleash · supercharge · game-changer · revolutionary · landscape · navigate · streamline · seamless · elevate · empower.
**Phrases:** "in today's fast-paced world" · "it's important to note" · "when it comes to" · "in order to" · "whether you're a … or a …" · "take your X to the next level" · "let's dive in".
**Shape:** em-dash chains · four-comma sentences · colon-titles · bold on every term · every line the same length · passive voice · no "you".

**The test:** read it aloud as the reader. If you'd never say it to a friend about your own problem, cut it.
